Svashasan OS Prediction Model

Campus EV Deployment Specification, Training Plan and Release Roadmap

Purpose: The engineering baseline for the Svashasan OS prediction subsystem: what it predicts, how it is built, trained, packaged and validated, and the 12-week path to a go/no-go decision on a supervised campus EV prototype.

Status note. Version 1.0, proposed engineering baseline. Target deployment: a supervised campus EV at up to 10 km/h. This specification defines design targets; it does not claim that a model has been trained, benchmarked, validated or certified against them.

Scope

Trajectory, behaviour, risk

Multi-agent trajectories, behaviour, uncertainty and risk for a campus EV at ≤10 km/h.

Model

~4M-parameter hybrid

GRU temporal encoder, light interaction attention, map context, four heads.

Deploy

INT8 on the edge

ONNX Runtime or TensorRT under ROS 2, p95 ≤30 ms, RAM ≤256 MB.

Decide

19 February 2027

Go/no-go on a supervised campus prototype after three release gates.

1. Executive Summary

Svashasan OS implements prediction as a compact, edge-deployable subsystem. It consumes tracked objects and ego-vehicle state from perception, predicts plausible future trajectories and behaviours, quantifies uncertainty, and supplies structured outputs to planning and safety control. The environment includes pedestrians, cyclists, service and parked vehicles, narrow paths, intersections, crossings, static obstacles and changing light and weather.

3. Operating Design Domain

The first release is restricted to a clearly documented campus operating domain: up to 10 km/h, supervised, on internal roads, paths and designated test routes, using only the sensors actually fitted and only the weather and light covered by validation evidence.

18. Release Gates

19. Roadmap and Final Verdict

Build a compact, modular, multi-task prediction subsystem, and share a scene representation with perception only after the prediction model has been independently validated. The release objective is not parameter count or headline accuracy: it is consistent, calibrated and timely predictions under the exact campus conditions, with robust handling of missing data and an independent safety-control path.
